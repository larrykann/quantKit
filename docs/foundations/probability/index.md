# Probability Foundations

## Why Probability for Finance?

Financial markets are fundamentally nondeterministic systems. Unlike deterministic calculations such as bond pricing with known parameters, stock prices involve inherent uncertainty that cannot be eliminated. We cannot know or measure all factors influencing market movements with precision.

This uncertainty necessitates probability theory as the mathematical framework for:

- Modeling uncertain outcomes
- Quantifying risk
- Making decisions under incomplete information
- Estimating unknown quantities from observed data

While some once hoped for a deterministic formula to predict stock prices, no such formula exists. Instead, probability analysis helps us reason systematically about financial uncertainty.

## Core Concepts

### Random Experiments
Repeatable activities with uncertain outcomes. Financial examples include capturing daily stock prices, measuring returns, or recording trading volumes.

### Sample Space (Ω)
The set of all possible outcomes of a random experiment. Provides the universe of what could happen.

### Events
Subsets of the sample space representing specific conditions or criteria of interest. Foundation for probability calculations.

### Random Variables
Functions that map outcomes to real numbers, enabling mathematical analysis of uncertain quantities. Transform qualitative events into quantitative measures.

### Probability Space
The formal mathematical structure $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)$ that ties everything together—sample space, event space, and probability measure.

## What You'll Learn

This section covers the foundational concepts of probability theory essential for quantitative finance:

- **Random experiments and sample spaces** - Defining uncertainty
- **Events and event operations** - Specifying conditions of interest
- **Probability definitions** - Classical, limiting ratio, and practical estimation
- **Random variables** - Quantifying uncertain outcomes  
- **Probability distributions** - PMF, PDF, and CDF for modeling likelihood
- **Sampling methods** - Generating random values for simulation

These foundations are prerequisites for understanding stochastic processes, which model how financial quantities evolve over time.
